TITLE
The Weight of the Adjoint: Which Symplectic Form a Neural Operator Preserves,
and Whether It Survives the Lift

AUTHORS  (arXiv wants "Forename Surname" separated by commas)
Sourav Sahu, Gareth O'Brien

ABSTRACT  (1615 characters -- arXiv's limit is 1920)
Structure-preserving neural operators are built on a question that is rarely stated explicitly: symplectic with respect to which inner product? On a non-uniform grid the discrete L^2 adjoint is K^*=W^-1K^TW, where W holds the quadrature weights, and the choice of W selects which symplectic form a gradient shear preserves. We make the choice explicit and measure it. The symplectic defect, computed from the autograd Jacobian, is exact (4x10^-16) in the form a shear was built from and O(1) in the others, in 1-D, 2-D and 3-D. Does matching the form to the grid help? On uniform grids the matched form has the lower error in 17 of 22 paired comparisons over five seeds, and none favours the other. On Chebyshev nodes, where the roles reverse, it is better in two of three comparisons and tied in the third; on a mapped grid where neither standard form is right it brings no consistent benefit. Because the weights also set the kernel's quadrature, the experiments cannot separate the form from the quadrature rule. Over 2x10^4-step rollouts, end-to-end symplectic maps stayed bounded in 60 of 63 runs (the exceptions: one mismatched-form model in 3-D), while a lifted variant with a non-symplectic linear lift and projection was bounded in 3 of 12. Bounded is not accurate: an identity map is bounded too, the error is comparable to the signal, and energy is off by over 100%, against 0.03% for the reference solver. A 783-run benchmark places the constructions among standard neural operators, and a worked example shows that a unit Jacobian determinant, which every shear has, does not make an update symplectic.
