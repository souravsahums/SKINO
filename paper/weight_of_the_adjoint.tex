\begin{abstract}
Structure-preserving neural operators are motivated by a premise that is almost
never tested directly: that endowing a learned solution operator with the
geometry of the underlying flow---symplecticity, in the Hamiltonian
case---improves accuracy or long-horizon stability. Testing it requires two
things the literature lacks: an operator whose structure is \emph{exact} rather
than nominal, and an instrument that \emph{measures} structure rather than
asserting it. We supply both. First, we show that on a non-uniform
Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto grid the discrete $L^2$ adjoint is
$K^{*}=W^{-1}K^{\top}W$, not $K^{\top}$, where $W$ collects the quadrature
weights; using the correct weighted adjoint, the gradient shear
$\Phi(q,p)=(q,\,p+K^{*}\rho(Kq))$ is \emph{exactly} symplectic for every
parameter value. On a uniform or periodic grid $W\propto I$ and the distinction
is invisible---which is why Fourier-parameterised symplectic operators never
encounter it. Second, we compute the symplectic defect
$\lVert WA-A^{\top}W\rVert_F/\lVert WA\rVert_F$ from the autograd Jacobian. The
instrument is decisive: our construction measures $2\times10^{-16}$ (machine
precision, resolution-independently), an otherwise identical twin using the
plain transpose measures $0.65$--$0.78$, and a widely-described
``pseudo-symplectic'' kernel operator measures $1.37$---that is, not symplectic
at all. Pairing the first two as trained models gives a clean experiment:
across $31$ configurations, $9$ PDEs and $3$ seeds at matched capacity, the
\emph{non}-symplectic twin is more accurate on \emph{all six} one-dimensional
problems (by $1.1\times$ to $4.3\times$), and neither variant gains rollout
stability. What does predict performance is matching the \emph{basis} to the
boundary conditions: on a Hamiltonian, non-periodic benchmark every Chebyshev
operator beats every Fourier operator by $3.0$--$4.9\times$, while Fourier
operators win four of five periodic problems. We conclude that the geometry of
the discretisation, not the geometry of the flow, is what earns its keep.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Neural operators learn maps between function spaces and have become standard
surrogates for parametric PDEs \citep{kovachki2023neural,li2021fno,lu2021deeponet}.
A prominent and fast-growing branch argues that such operators should additionally
respect the \emph{geometric structure} of the dynamics they emulate: symplecticity
for Hamiltonian systems, or the metriplectic split for systems with dissipation.
The argument is appealing and has a strong pedigree in finite dimensions
\citep{greydanus2019hnn,jin2020sympnets,cranmer2020lnn,zhong2020symoden}, and
2026 saw it carried into function space by several independent groups
\citep{makara2026sno,sulskis2026generic,zhang2026legonet,varghese2026pisonet}.

The premise underlying all of this work is rarely isolated. Papers typically
compare a structured operator against an \emph{unstructured} baseline that differs
in many ways at once---parameterisation, capacity, training recipe---and report
that the structured model is better. Such a comparison cannot separate the
contribution of the geometric prior from everything else that changed. Worse,
whether a model is actually structure-preserving is usually argued on paper and
never measured on the trained network.

This paper isolates the premise. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A correction and a construction (\S\ref{sec:method}).} We identify
an error that is easy to make and hard to see: on a non-uniform grid, the $L^2$
adjoint is not the matrix transpose. We give the correct weighted adjoint and
prove that the resulting gradient shear is exactly symplectic on a
Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto grid for \emph{any} kernel and \emph{any} pointwise
nonlinearity (Theorem~\ref{thm:sacheb}). This yields \sacheb{}, to our knowledge
the first exactly symplectic neural operator on a non-periodic domain.

\item \textbf{An instrument (\S\ref{sec:defect}).} We measure the symplectic
defect directly from the autograd Jacobian. It certifies \sacheb{} at
$2\times10^{-16}$, shows the weighted adjoint is \emph{necessary} (plain
transpose: $0.65$--$0.78$), and refutes the ``pseudo-symplectic'' label attached
to a Chebyshev kernel-integral operator we also study
($1.37$; \S\ref{sec:defect-results}).

\item \textbf{A controlled refutation (\S\ref{sec:ablation}).} Because
\sacheb{} and its wrong-adjoint twin \naive{} differ \emph{only} in whether they
are symplectic, we can test the premise directly. The non-symplectic twin is
more accurate on all six 1-D problems, and the advertised long-horizon benefit
does not materialise either.

\item \textbf{A positive result (\S\ref{sec:basis}).} Basis--boundary matching
predicts the winner. We introduce a Hamiltonian \emph{non-periodic} benchmark on
which Chebyshev operators beat every Fourier operator by $3.0$--$4.9\times$,
while the ordering reverses on periodic problems.

\item \textbf{A benchmark (\S\ref{sec:setup}).} $31$ configurations $\times$ $9$
PDEs $\times$ $3$ seeds at genuinely matched capacity ($\approx$25k parameters),
with prediction mode and training noise \emph{controlled} rather than confounded,
and with physical verdicts rather than RMS alone.
\end{enumerate}

We stress what we are \emph{not} claiming. We do not claim structure preservation
is useless in general; we claim that in this protocol, on these problems, at
matched capacity, it does not pay---and that the burden of measurement, not
assertion, should fall on the claim.

\section{Related work}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Neural operators.} The Fourier Neural Operator parameterises the
integral kernel by a truncated set of Fourier multipliers \citep{li2021fno},
inheriting discretisation invariance and $O(N\log N)$ cost; DeepONet factorises
the operator into branch and trunk networks \citep{lu2021deeponet}; the general
framework and universality results are given by \citet{kovachki2023neural}.
Tensorised \citep{kossaifi2023mgtfno} and U-Net-augmented \citep{wen2022ufno}
variants improve parameter efficiency and accuracy. A recent practitioner-oriented
account of FNO's assumptions and failure modes is given by
\citet{duruisseaux2026fnoexplained}, and reference implementations by
\citet{kossaifi2024library}. Physics-informed variants add PDE residual terms to
the loss \citep{raissi2019pinn,li2023pino}; these are \emph{soft} constraints and
give no algebraic guarantee.

\paragraph{Structure preservation in finite dimensions.} Hamiltonian Neural
Networks \citep{greydanus2019hnn} and Lagrangian Neural Networks
\citep{cranmer2020lnn} learn the generating function; SympNets
\citep{jin2020sympnets} and Symplectic ODE-Net \citep{zhong2020symoden}
parameterise symplectic maps directly. These act on $\R^{2d}$ and depend on a
fixed discretisation, so they are not operators in the function-space sense.

\paragraph{Structure-preserving neural operators.} \citet{makara2026sno}
introduce the Symplectic Neural Operator (SNO), composing symplectic shear blocks
whose update fields are gradients of learned energy functionals, realised with
self-adjoint Fourier layers on the torus; they prove a modified-Hamiltonian
stability result. \citet{sulskis2026generic} embed the full GENERIC
\citep{grmela1997generic,ottinger2005beyond} degeneracy structure into an FNO
using projected Fourier multipliers, extending finite-dimensional bracket
learning \citep{zhang2022gfinns}. \citet{zhang2026legonet} compose
structure-preserving blocks on boundary-adapted spectral representations, and
\citet{varghese2026pisonet} apply a conditional symplectic operator to optimal
control. Our work is complementary and adversarial: we take the strongest form of
the symplectic premise, realise it exactly on a domain the Fourier constructions
cannot address, and then measure whether it pays.

\paragraph{Positioning.} \citet{makara2026sno} state explicitly that their
formulation applies to \emph{strong canonical symplectic Hilbert phase spaces},
and that extensions beyond that setting require further geometric machinery. The
non-periodic case is precisely where the adjoint subtlety of
\S\ref{sec:method} appears, and is the setting of our benchmark
\S\ref{sec:basis}. Auxiliary ingredients we use---Lie-equivariant lifting
\citep{finzi2020lieconv} and FiLM conditioning \citep{perez2018film}---are
standard and not claimed as contributions.

\section{Background: discrete symplectic structure on a non-uniform grid}
\label{sec:background}
% ============================================================================

Let $\Omega=[-1,1]$ be discretised on the $N+1$ Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto (CGL)
nodes $x_j=\cos(j\pi/N)$. Functions are represented by their nodal values, and
the physical $L^2$ inner product is approximated by Clenshaw--Curtis quadrature
\citep{trefethen2000spectral},
\begin{equation}
\iprod{f}{g}_W \;=\; \int_\Omega f g\,dx \;\approx\; f^{\top} W g,
\qquad W=\mathrm{diag}(w_0,\dots,w_N),\quad w_j>0 .
\label{eq:ip}
\end{equation}
The weights $w_j$ are \emph{not} constant: they vary by a factor of order $N$
between the boundary and the interior of the grid. This single fact drives
everything that follows.

Phase space is $\mathbb{P}=\mathcal{H}_q\oplus\mathcal{H}_p$ with
$\mathcal{H}=(\R^{N+1},\iprod{\cdot}{\cdot}_W)$, carrying the canonical form
\begin{equation}
\omega_W(z,z') = \iprod{q}{p'}_W - \iprod{p}{q'}_W = z^{\top}\Omega z',
\qquad
\Omega=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & W \\ -W & 0\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Weighted adjoint]
For a linear operator $K$, the adjoint with respect to \eqref{eq:ip} is the
unique $K^{*}$ with $\iprod{Kf}{g}_W=\iprod{f}{K^{*}g}_W$ for all $f,g$.
Expanding, $f^{\top}K^{\top}Wg=f^{\top}WK^{*}g$ for all $f,g$, hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;K^{*}=W^{-1}K^{\top}W\;}
\label{eq:adjoint}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why this is invisible in a Fourier operator]
\label{rem:invisible}
On a uniform or periodic grid the quadrature weight is a single scalar,
$W=cI$, so \eqref{eq:adjoint} collapses to $K^{*}=K^{\top}$ and the weighted
adjoint coincides with the matrix transpose. Every construction built on the
torus therefore gets the adjoint right \emph{by accident}. On CGL nodes it does
not, and substituting $K^{\top}$ for $K^{*}$ silently destroys the geometry one
is trying to impose. This is the eponymous ``weight of the adjoint''.
\end{remark}

The following elementary proposition is the criterion we will use throughout,
and it is also where a common error enters.

\begin{proposition}[Shear criterion]
\label{prop:shear}
Let $\Phi(q,p)=(q,\,p+F(q))$ with $A=DF(q)$. Then
\begin{equation}
(D\Phi)^{\top}\Omega\,(D\Phi)-\Omega
=\begin{pmatrix} WA-A^{\top}W & 0\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:defect-identity}
\end{equation}
so $\Phi$ preserves $\omega_W$ \emph{if and only if} $A$ is self-adjoint in $W$,
i.e.\ $WA=A^{\top}W$. The analogous statement holds for the transposed shear
$(q,p)\mapsto(q+G(p),p)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$D\Phi=\begin{psmallmatrix} I&0\\ A&I\end{psmallmatrix}$. Then
$\Omega D\Phi=\begin{psmallmatrix} WA& W\\ -W&0\end{psmallmatrix}$ and
$(D\Phi)^{\top}\Omega D\Phi
=\begin{psmallmatrix} I&A^{\top}\\ 0&I\end{psmallmatrix}
 \begin{psmallmatrix} WA& W\\ -W&0\end{psmallmatrix}
=\begin{psmallmatrix} WA-A^{\top}W& W\\ -W&0\end{psmallmatrix}$.
Subtracting $\Omega$ gives \eqref{eq:defect-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Volume preservation is not symplecticity]
\label{rem:volume}
$\det D\Phi=1$ for \emph{every} shear, regardless of $A$. Unit Jacobian
determinant is Liouville volume preservation, which is strictly weaker than
preservation of $\omega_W$: by Proposition~\ref{prop:shear} the latter requires
the additional algebraic condition $WA=A^{\top}W$. Conflating the two is, in our
experience, the single most common way a ``symplectic'' architecture turns out
not to be one; we exhibit a concrete instance in \S\ref{sec:defect-results}.
\end{remark}

\section{Method: exactly symplectic shears on a Chebyshev grid}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{The construction}

Let $K$ be any bounded linear operator (in our implementation, a low-rank
Chebyshev kernel; see \S\ref{sec:impl}), let $R\in C^1(\R)$ with $\rho=R'$, and
define the energy functional and its $W$-gradient
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(q)=\int_\Omega R\big((Kq)(x)\big)\,dx,
\qquad
F(q) \;:=\; \nabla_W\mathcal{E}(q) \;=\; K^{*}\rho(Kq),
\label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
with $K^{*}$ the weighted adjoint \eqref{eq:adjoint}. The \sacheb{} block
alternates the two shears
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{\uparrow}(q,p)=(q,\;p+F_\theta(q)),
\qquad
\Phi^{\downarrow}(q,p)=(q+G_\vartheta(p),\;p),
\label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
each of the form \eqref{eq:field} with its own parameters.

\begin{theorem}[Exact symplecticity on a non-periodic grid]
\label{thm:sacheb}
For any parameters of $K$, any $R\in C^1$, and any resolution $N$, the map
$\Phi^{\uparrow}$ in \eqref{eq:block} satisfies
$(\Phi^{\uparrow})^{*}\omega_W=\omega_W$ exactly. The same holds for
$\Phi^{\downarrow}$, and hence for any finite alternating composition
$\Phi^{\uparrow}_{\theta_L}\circ\Phi^{\downarrow}_{\vartheta_L}\circ\cdots\circ
\Phi^{\uparrow}_{\theta_1}\circ\Phi^{\downarrow}_{\vartheta_1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $D=\mathrm{diag}\big(\rho'((Kq)_j)\big)$. Differentiating
\eqref{eq:field}, $A=DF=K^{*}DK$. Using \eqref{eq:adjoint},
\[
WA \;=\; W\,(W^{-1}K^{\top}W)\,D\,K \;=\; K^{\top}WDK,
\]
\[
A^{\top}W \;=\; K^{\top}D^{\top}(K^{*})^{\top}W
\;=\; K^{\top}D\,(W^{-1}K^{\top}W)^{\top}W
\;=\; K^{\top}D\,W K W^{-1} W
\;=\; K^{\top}DWK .
\]
Both $W$ and $D$ are diagonal, hence commute: $WD=DW$. Therefore
$WA=K^{\top}WDK=K^{\top}DWK=A^{\top}W$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:shear} gives
$(D\Phi^{\uparrow})^{\top}\Omega D\Phi^{\uparrow}=\Omega$. Compositions of
symplectic maps are symplectic.
\end{proof}

Three features of Theorem~\ref{thm:sacheb} are worth emphasising. It is
\emph{unconditional} in the parameters---the guarantee survives any optimiser and
any amount of training, and is not an approximation that degrades with step size.
It is \emph{resolution-independent}: $W$ and the basis are recomputed at whatever
grid the operator is evaluated on, so the same trained weights are exactly
symplectic at every $N$. And it is \emph{non-periodic}: the construction lives on
CGL nodes and imposes no periodicity, which is exactly the regime the Fourier
constructions of \citet{makara2026sno} exclude.

\begin{proposition}[Necessity of the weighted adjoint]
\label{prop:necessity}
Replace $K^{*}$ in \eqref{eq:field} by the plain transpose $K^{\top}$, giving
$\tilde F(q)=K^{\top}\rho(Kq)$ and $\tilde A=K^{\top}DK$. Then
$W\tilde A-\tilde A^{\top}W = W K^{\top}DK - K^{\top}DKW$, which vanishes for all
$K,D$ only if $W$ commutes with $K^{\top}DK$ for all such products---in
particular it holds identically when $W\propto I$, and fails in general
otherwise. Hence on a uniform grid the two constructions coincide, while on CGL
nodes the transpose variant is not symplectic.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity} is what makes the experiment of
\S\ref{sec:ablation} possible: it furnishes an architecture that is identical to
\sacheb{} in every respect except that it lacks the property under test. We call
it \naive{}.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

We take $K$ to be a rank-$R$ separable Chebyshev kernel,
\begin{equation}
(Kv)(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\varphi_r(x)\,M_r\,\iprod{\psi_r}{v}_W ,
\qquad
(K^{*}g)(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\psi_r(x)\,M_r^{\top}\,\iprod{\varphi_r}{g}_W ,
\label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
where $M_r$ mixes channels. The second identity is the weighted adjoint of the
first---verified by expanding both sides of $\iprod{Kv}{g}_W=\iprod{v}{K^{*}g}_W$
---so in the low-rank form the adjoint is obtained simply by \emph{swapping
$\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi$ and transposing $M$}, and no matrix inverse is ever
formed. The basis functions $\varphi_r,\psi_r$ are stored as \emph{Chebyshev
coefficients} and evaluated on whatever CGL grid is requested, which is what
makes the operator resolution-agnostic. \naive{} is obtained by substituting
uniform weights for $w$ in the adjoint contraction only.

\section{Measuring structure: the symplectic defect}
\label{sec:defect}
% ============================================================================

Proposition~\ref{prop:shear} turns ``is this block symplectic?'' into a number.
For a trained (or randomly initialised) shear field $F$ we form the dense
Jacobian $A=DF$ by reverse-mode differentiation and report the scale-invariant
\emph{relative symplectic defect}
\begin{equation}
\defect(F) \;=\; \frac{\lVert WA-A^{\top}W\rVert_F}{\lVert WA\rVert_F}
\;\in\;[0,\infty),
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
which is zero if and only if the block preserves $\omega_W$. Because
$\defect$ is computed from the model's own Jacobian, it is independent of the
architecture's documentation, and applies unchanged to any $(q,p)$-shear
architecture. We regard this instrument as a contribution in its own right: it
converts a claim that is usually made in prose into one that can be falsified in
one line of code.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Problems.} Nine PDEs spanning linear transport, diffusion,
oscillation, shocks, dispersion, and multi-dimensional conservative dynamics:
advection, heat, \texttt{wave1d}, \textbf{\texttt{wave1d\_dir}}, Burgers, KdV,
\texttt{wave2d} ($32^2$), \texttt{wave3d} ($16^3$) and \texttt{ns2d}
($64^2$, vorticity-form Navier--Stokes). All but \texttt{wave1d\_dir} are
periodic.

\texttt{wave1d\_dir} is introduced here as the discriminating rung: the wave
equation on $[0,L]$ with homogeneous \emph{Dirichlet} boundaries, discretised on
a uniform grid with centred differences and advanced by symplectic leap-frog,
with initial conditions formed from a truncated Chebyshev series multiplied by
the envelope $x(L-x)$. This construction mirrors the data-generation protocol of
\citet{makara2026sno}, so the comparison is made on the concurrent method's own
terms. It is simultaneously Hamiltonian (so symplecticity is meaningful) and
non-periodic (so a Fourier parameterisation is structurally mismatched: the
periodic extension of the envelope has a derivative discontinuity at the
boundary). The reference solver conserves energy to $2.3\times10^{-4}$ over
$500$ steps and holds the boundary values at exactly zero.

\paragraph{Operators.} Ten families, all parameter-matched to
$\approx$\,25k: \sacheb{} and \naive{} (this work); \ckino{}, a Chebyshev
kernel-integral operator with a St\"ormer--Verlet-style $(q,p)$ update, together
with its exactly-constrained and structure-removed ablations; SNO
\citep{makara2026sno} and GENERIC-FNO \citep{sulskis2026generic}, which we
re-implement; and FNO \citep{li2021fno}, T-FNO
\citep{kossaifi2023mgtfno}, U-FNO \citep{wen2022ufno}, U-Net, DeepONet
\citep{lu2021deeponet} and a Transformer operator as unstructured references.

\paragraph{Design.} Configurations cross each family with prediction mode
$\{$recursive, non-recursive \texttt{seq2seq}$\}$, training noise
$\{$none, $2\%\}$ and an optional PDE-residual loss, giving $31$ configurations
in 1-D. Crucially this yields a $2\times2$ factorial---$\{$Fourier,
Chebyshev$\}$ basis $\times$ $\{$exactly symplectic, not$\}$---realised as
SNO / \sacheb{} / FNO / \ckino{}, plus the \naive{} adjoint ablation that varies
symplecticity with everything else held fixed. Each cell is run with $3$ seeds on
a single hardware (NVIDIA Tesla T4), giving $657$ training runs.

\paragraph{Metrics.} We report relative RMS at rollout checkpoints, but not only
that: a model can post a middling RMS while being physically dead. We
additionally compute amplitude ratio, pattern correlation, spectral error and
invariant drift, and collapse them into a \emph{verdict}
(good $\to$ degraded $\to$ decorrelated $\to$ amplitude-collapse $\to$ blow-up)
and a \emph{usable horizon} (last step with correlation $\ge0.9$ and amplitude
ratio in $[0.7,1.4]$).

\subsection{The instrument certifies one construction and refutes another}
\label{sec:defect-results}

Three conclusions follow. (i) Theorem~\ref{thm:sacheb} holds numerically: the
defect sits at machine precision and does not grow with resolution. (ii) The
weighted adjoint is \emph{necessary}, not cosmetic---replacing $K^{*}$ by
$K^{\top}$ leaves an $O(1)$ violation, confirming
Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity}. (iii) The \ckino{} kernel shear, of the kind
often described as ``pseudo-symplectic'' because its Jacobian is triangular with
unit diagonal, has defect $\approx1.37$: it is volume-preserving but \emph{not}
symplectic, exactly the conflation flagged in Remark~\ref{rem:volume}. Its kernel
\eqref{eq:lowrank} uses independent $\varphi_r,\psi_r$ and an unconstrained
channel mix, so nothing forces $k(x,y)=k(y,x)^{\top}$.

Point (iii) matters methodologically. Ablating a ``symplectic'' component that
was never symplectic tests nothing about symplecticity. Only the \sacheb{} /
\naive{} pair does.

\subsection{The controlled experiment: exact symplecticity is a cost}
\label{sec:ablation}

\sacheb{} and \naive{} share an architecture, a parameter count and a training
recipe; they differ only in the adjoint, and therefore only in whether they are
symplectic ($2\times10^{-16}$ versus $\approx0.7$). Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
compares them at matched capacity.

The non-symplectic twin wins \emph{all six} problems, by factors from $1.07$ to
$4.30$. The effect is not confined to accuracy: the long-horizon stability that
motivates structure preservation also fails to appear---on
\texttt{wave1d\_dir} both recursive arms diverge (relative RMS clipped at
$10^{3}$, usable horizon $33$ steps). Figure~\ref{fig:growth} shows the
corresponding error-growth behaviour across the study.

We interpret this as a capacity-allocation effect rather than a numerical
artefact. The constraint $WA=A^{\top}W$ removes roughly half the degrees of
freedom of the shear Jacobian; at a fixed budget of $\approx$25k parameters that
expressivity is evidently worth more than the geometric invariant, on horizons
where the invariant has not yet had time to matter.

\subsection{Supporting axes}

\paragraph{Capacity.} A budget sweep from 6k to 400k parameters shows that the
Chebyshev family's failure on Burgers is a \emph{representational} ceiling, not
an undertraining artefact: the error is flat at $\approx0.25$ across a
$60\times$ range of capacity. Likewise the recursive KdV instability persists at
every budget. Neither failure is fixed by scale.

\paragraph{Discretisation invariance.} Training at $N=64$ and evaluating the same
weights at $N=128$, the Fourier operators are exactly invariant (degradation
ratio $1.00$--$1.02$; \citealp{li2021fno,kovachki2023neural}), whereas the
Chebyshev operator with a fixed-stencil lifting layer degrades by $57\times$ on
advection and $3.0\times$ on KdV. Replacing the lift with a spectral-derivative
lift restores exact invariance ($1.00$ on all three problems) but costs one to
two orders of magnitude of accuracy at the training grid. Chebyshev operators
thus face an invariance-versus-accuracy trade-off that the Fourier family does
not. We report this as a limitation of our own construction.

\paragraph{Inference speed.} Against the spectral reference solver the operators
lose on cheap linear problems (fixed overhead dominates: $0.07$--$0.37\times$ on
advection, heat and \texttt{wave1d}) and win only where the reference is
expensive ($3.2\times$ for FNO on Burgers, $7.7\times$ on KdV; $2.3\times$ for
\ckino{} on KdV). ``Neural operators are faster'' is a statement about the
solver being replaced, not about the operator.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Figure~\ref{fig:verdict} shows why RMS alone is
insufficient: several configurations with unremarkable RMS are physically dead or
amplitude-collapsed, and only the amplitude/correlation decomposition separates
them.

\section{Where our contribution is stronger}
\label{sec:better}
% ============================================================================

It is worth stating plainly what this work does better than the alternatives, and
where it does not.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Exact symplecticity off the torus.} \sacheb{} is, to our knowledge,
the first neural operator that is exactly symplectic on a \emph{non-periodic}
domain, certified at $2\times10^{-16}$ and resolution-independently
(Table~\ref{tab:defect}). \citet{makara2026sno} achieve exactness on the torus
and state the non-canonical/non-periodic extension as open; our
Theorem~\ref{thm:sacheb} closes the non-periodic half of that gap, and identifies
the weighted adjoint as the obstruction that makes it non-trivial
(Remark~\ref{rem:invisible}).

\item \textbf{Accuracy on non-periodic Hamiltonian problems.} On
\texttt{wave1d\_dir}, evaluated on a data protocol matching
\citet{makara2026sno}'s own, the Chebyshev family beats every Fourier operator:
$3.0\times$ over the best Fourier model and $4.9\times$ over SNO
(Table~\ref{tab:basis}). The same basis advantage appears on an elliptic Darcy
problem with Dirichlet boundaries, where our operator attains $8.3\times$ lower
relative $L^2$ error than FNO overall and $17\times$ lower error \emph{at the
boundary}, while transferring zero-shot from a $64^2$ to a $128^2$ grid.

\item \textbf{Multi-dimensional conservative problems.} Against the
parameter-matched FNO, our Chebyshev operator is $5.3\times$ better on
\texttt{wave2d}, $4.6\times$ better on \texttt{wave3d} (where FNO's usable
horizon collapses to $2$ steps against $150$), and $1.4\times$ better on
\texttt{ns2d}.

\item \textbf{Falsifiability.} We contribute an instrument \eqref{eq:defect} that
makes structure-preservation claims checkable, and we apply it first to our own
architecture---where it shows a previously asserted guarantee to be false
(\S\ref{sec:defect-results}). We know of no comparable audit in this literature.

\item \textbf{Experimental control.} Prediction mode and training noise are
varied as explicit factors rather than bundled into a single ``our method''
configuration, at matched capacity, with three seeds and physical verdicts. This
is what allows the symplecticity ablation of \S\ref{sec:ablation} to be read
causally.
\end{enumerate}

Conversely, we are worse where the basis is wrong for the problem: on smooth
periodic problems with shocks (Burgers) the Chebyshev basis has a hard
representational ceiling that no budget removes, and our operator lacks the
automatic discretisation invariance of a Fourier parameterisation.

% ============================================================================
\section{Limitations}
% ============================================================================

\textbf{Scope of the negative result.} Our refutation is specific: matched
$\approx$25k capacity, rollouts of a few hundred steps, nine PDEs, one hardware.
Symplectic integrators are classically motivated by behaviour over very long
horizons; it remains possible that the ordering reverses at horizons well beyond
those studied here, or at much larger capacity where the constraint costs
proportionally less.

\textbf{Re-implementations.} SNO and GENERIC-FNO are our re-implementations from
the published descriptions, in 1-D only, and were not tuned to the authors'
protocols. Consequently the multi-dimensional comparisons are against FNO alone.
Our GENERIC-FNO re-implementation additionally failed to train: parameterising
the PSD multiplier as $M=b(k)^2$ with $b$ initialised at zero places it at a
saddle ($\partial M/\partial b=2b=0$), so the dissipative channel never
activates and the remaining skew update grows under the explicit step. This is a
defect of our implementation, not evidence about the published method, and no
conclusion should be drawn from those rows.

\textbf{Canonical structure only.} \sacheb{} assumes a canonical $(q,p)$ split.
Non-canonical Hamiltonian PDEs---KdV under the Gardner bracket being the obvious
example---are not covered, and a learned Poisson structure with a Jacobi
guarantee remains open; \citet{sulskis2026generic} note the same gap for the
metriplectic case.

\textbf{Scale.} Domains are small ($32$--$64$ points in 1-D, $32^2$, $16^3$,
$64^2$), which is adequate to resolve the orderings reported but is not a
deployment benchmark.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================

We set out to test, rather than assume, the premise that geometric structure
helps a learned operator. Doing so required fixing a subtle error---on a
non-uniform grid the $L^2$ adjoint is $W^{-1}K^{\top}W$, not $K^{\top}$---which
yields the first exactly symplectic neural operator on a non-periodic domain, and
building an instrument that measures symplecticity instead of asserting it. The
instrument immediately showed that a widely-used ``pseudo-symplectic'' block is
not symplectic at all, and the construction made a clean ablation possible for
the first time. The answer that came back is negative: at matched capacity, an
exactly symplectic operator is beaten by its own non-symplectic twin on every
1-D problem we tried, and gains no stability. What does predict performance is
mundane and actionable---match the basis to the boundary conditions. We think the
right conclusion is not that structure preservation is worthless, but that in
this literature it has been argued far more often than it has been measured, and
that the measurement is cheap.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All experiments are driven by a single harness with
fixed seeds; the symplectic-defect instrument is a self-contained script, and
every number in this paper is produced by it or by the benchmark runner.

\section{Retraction of a previously asserted guarantee}
\label{app:retraction}
% ============================================================================

The \ckino{} operator studied here was originally documented with an exact
symplecticity theorem. We retract it, and record the error because it is
instructive and, we suspect, not unique to us.

The withdrawn argument ran: each St\"ormer--Verlet substep has a Jacobian that is
triangular with unit diagonal blocks, hence determinant $1$, hence preserves
$\omega$. The step from ``determinant $1$'' to ``preserves $\omega$'' is invalid.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:shear}, $\det D\Phi=1$ holds for every shear, while
preservation of $\omega_W$ additionally requires $WA=A^{\top}W$. The operator's
kernel \eqref{eq:lowrank} uses independent $\varphi_r,\psi_r$ and an
unconstrained channel-mixing matrix, so $A$ is not self-adjoint; the measured
defect is $\approx1.37$ (Table~\ref{tab:defect}). A corollary energy-drift bound,
whose proof invoked the modified-Hamiltonian machinery of backward error analysis
\citep{hairer2006gni}, is retracted with it: that machinery requires
symplecticity, and volume preservation alone does not imply the existence of a
modified Hamiltonian.

\section{The weighted adjoint in the low-rank basis}
\label{app:lowrank}
% ============================================================================

We verify the adjoint pair \eqref{eq:lowrank}. With
$\alpha_r=\iprod{\psi_r}{v}_W$ and $(Kv)(x)=\sum_r\varphi_r(x)M_r\alpha_r$,
\[
\iprod{Kv}{g}_W
=\sum_r (M_r\alpha_r)^{\top}\iprod{\varphi_r}{g}_W
=\sum_r \alpha_r^{\top}\big(M_r^{\top}\iprod{\varphi_r}{g}_W\big)
=\Big\langle v,\;\sum_r \psi_r\,M_r^{\top}\iprod{\varphi_r}{g}_W\Big\rangle_W,
\]
which is exactly $\iprod{v}{K^{*}g}_W$ for $K^{*}$ as given in
\eqref{eq:lowrank}. Hence in this parameterisation the weighted adjoint is
obtained by exchanging the roles of $\varphi$ and $\psi$ and transposing the
channel mixing---no inverse of $W$ is ever formed, and the construction remains
$O(RN)$.
